Algorithm ES256 Like I am 5

 You (the issuer) have a special stamp — that's the private key. Only you have this stamp. Nobody else in the world has an identical one.

  You write a letter (the JWT payload — emails, courses, purpose, etc.) and press your stamp into wax on the envelope. That stamped wax seal is the signature.

  You send the letter (JWT = letter + seal) to the receiving app via the user's browser.

  ---
  The receiving party has a photo of your stamp — that's the public key. You gave them this photo once beforehand.

  When they receive the letter, they look at the wax seal and compare it to the photo. If it matches, they know:
  - The letter really came from you (not a forger)
  - Nobody tampered with the letter contents after you sealed it

  They cannot make new stamps from the photo — the public key can only verify, never create signatures.

  ---
  The specific details of ES256:

  - E = Elliptic Curve — a type of math used to create the stamp. Makes the stamp very hard to forge even with small keys.
  - S = Signing — it's used to sign things (not encrypt).
  - 256 = uses SHA-256 to create a fingerprint of the letter before stamping it. This means even a tiny change to the letter would produce a completely different fingerprint, and the
  stamp wouldn't match.
  - P-256 = the specific "shape" of the elliptic curve used — it's a well-known, standardized curve.

  In practice:
  1. Private key (your stamp): a 256-bit secret number
  2. Public key (photo of stamp): a point on the P-256 curve derived from the private key
  3. Signing: hash the payload with SHA-256, then use the private key to produce a signature
  4. Verifying: use the public key + the signature + the payload to check if they all match
